The Land Owner’s Guide — Taxes & Acreage

Building a Home on Land With an Ag Exemption

Building a house on land with agricultural valuation usually triggers rollback taxes on the home site: the tax difference for the previous three years on the land that changes use. It’s a predictable cost, and with a little planning it belongs in your budget from the start, not as a surprise after move-in.

The Short Version

If your land has agricultural or wildlife valuation, building a home on it takes the house site out of qualifying use. When that happens, the appraisal district can charge rollback taxes: the difference between what you paid under ag valuation and what you would have paid at market value, for the previous three years, on the land that changed use.

It's not a penalty for doing something wrong. It's a known cost of converting land, and it belongs in your build budget from day one.

How Ag Valuation Works

Texas lets qualifying land be taxed on what it produces as farm, ranch, or wildlife land instead of what it would sell for. In fast-growing parts of Denton County, that gap is enormous. A tract worth hundreds of thousands of dollars at market can carry a taxable ag value of a small fraction of that.

That low value is the reason so much acreage around Krum, Sanger, Ponder, Pilot Point, and Justin stays in hay or cattle until someone is ready to build.

What Triggers a Rollback

A rollback is triggered when land with special valuation changes to a use that doesn't qualify. Building a home is the most common one. The key points:

  • It applies to the portion that changes use, typically the home site, yard, and driveway, not necessarily the whole tract.
  • The lookback is three years. The Legislature shortened it from five years in 2019.
  • The interest charge is gone. Rollback taxes used to carry interest. A 2021 law removed it.
  • The remaining acreage can often keep its valuation if it continues to meet the use and size requirements.

Rules on minimum acreage and qualifying use are set locally by the appraisal district, so confirm the specifics with the district for your county before you plan around them.

A Simple Way to Estimate It

You can get a rough idea before you call anyone:

  1. Decide roughly how much land the home site will take. On most acreage builds, that's one to a few acres.
  2. Look up the parcel on the appraisal district site. It shows both the market value and the ag value.
  3. Find the per-acre difference between market and ag value, then multiply by the acres converting.
  4. Multiply that by the total tax rate on the parcel, then by three years.

That's a ballpark, not a bill. The appraisal district calculates the actual amount. The point is to know whether you're looking at a few hundred dollars or a number that belongs in your loan.

Planning the Home Site to Protect the Rest

Where you put the house matters for more than the view. A well-planned home site keeps the converted area compact and leaves the rest of the tract usable as ag land. That usually means:

  • Keeping the house, septic field, barn, and driveway grouped instead of spread across the property.
  • Fencing the home site separately from pasture or hay ground.
  • Continuing the qualifying use on the remaining acreage without interruption.

Talk with the appraisal district and your tax advisor before construction starts. A short conversation up front is much easier than sorting it out after the house is built.

Other Things That Change When You Build

  • Homestead exemption: Once you live in the home, file for the residence homestead exemption on the house and home site.
  • The house is new value: Expect a significant jump in appraised value the first full year after completion.
  • Lenders will ask: Construction lenders often want to know about rollback exposure before closing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many years of rollback taxes do I owe in Texas?

For land under agricultural valuation, the rollback covers the previous three years on the portion of land that changes use. It was five years before the Legislature shortened it in 2019.

Is there interest on rollback taxes?

Not anymore. A 2021 law eliminated the interest that used to be added to rollback taxes when land changes use.

Do I lose the ag exemption on all my land if I build a house?

Not necessarily. The rollback applies to the land that changes use. The rest of the tract can often keep its valuation if it still meets the appraisal district's requirements.

Who calculates the rollback amount?

The county appraisal district determines the amount after the change in use. You can estimate it ahead of time using the market and ag values shown on your parcel record.

This guide is general information, not tax or legal advice. Rules and local requirements change, so confirm the details for your property with your appraisal district and a qualified tax professional.
